01 · Core Emotional Truth

Core Story
Got Strong
Emotional
Foundation

The brand already has million-dollar potential because of its core story. It just needs the right positioning.

Directly from successbykersaint.com

"What started as a reminder to stay focused has grown into a national movement. SuccessByKersaint is more than just apparel — it's a way to inspire, empower, and remind everyone that change is possible. Together, we're capturing young minds before negativity takes hold, one T-shirt at a time."

How the site language maps to the brand architecture

→ Core Story / Emotional Truth
"Grown into a national movement"
→ Movement Layer
"Capturing young minds before negativity takes hold"
→ Mission Layer
"Stand Up Create Change Every Single Second"

The strategy does not contradict the site — it amplifies it. The site says it carefully. The strategy says it with the full weight of Rodney's real story behind it.

Rodney Kersaint grew up in Brooklyn, NY and Willingboro, NJ — raised by his mother Juliette, who worked multiple jobs to raise four children alone. At 8 years old, Rodney and a friend were forced to their knees at gunpoint while a neighbor played Russian roulette. That moment changed everything.

The anger that followed led to removal from high school, boarding school, getting kicked out of the family home, and running with the wrong crowd. He lost friends to jail and death.

In 2008, Rodney joined the United States Navy and was stationed in San Diego. The change of scenery changed his mindset. He put the word SUCCESS on a T-shirt — not as a fashion statement, but as a personal reminder to stay focused. It became a local hit, landing him a feature on Channel 8 News with Larry Himmel.

S.U.C.C.E.S.S. — Stand Up. Create Change. Every Single Second.

This is not a clothing line. It is a movement built to capture young minds before negative influences do — and to break the cycle of negativity one T-shirt at a time.

02 · Audience Psychology

Buyer
Persona

TRUTH

The audience spans three connected groups: youth in schools Rodney speaks to directly, college students navigating who they're becoming, and aspiring young adults trying to figure out life after setbacks. All three are the same person at different stages — and Rodney has lived all three.

TRUTH

They don't buy motivation. They buy mirrors. The 19-year-old first-gen college student, the kid who just got kicked out, the 26-year-old starting over — they wear SUCCESS because it reflects who they're fighting to become, not who the world says they are.

TRUTH

This audience is deeply loyal to brands built by someone who actually lived their story. Rodney survived Russian roulette at 8, lost friends to jail and death, rebuilt in the Navy, and built a movement from a single T-shirt. That is not a brand story — it is their story.

TRUTH

College students and aspiring youth in the 18–30 range are the highest-sharing demographic on TikTok and Instagram. When they connect with a brand, they don't just buy — they recruit. One authentic post reaches an entire campus, dorm, or group chat.

GAP

Parents, educators, counselors, and community leaders are an untapped buyer. They attend Rodney's school talks and want to bring the movement back to their students. A bulk and institutional orders page targeting this group does not exist yet — and it should.

04 · Website Audit

What needs
to change.

Homepage
✕

Kill the SEO headline. "Motivational Apparel & Inspirational T-Shirts" is a category description, not a brand statement. Replace with our new hero copy.

✕

Kill the missing CTA. Website visitors arrive with no directed next step. One hero button: "Join the Movement" → hero product collection.

✕

Kill the hidden opt-in — make it a pop-up. A lead capture exists but it requires visitors to find and click a gift icon. Most won't. A pop-up opt-in that triggers automatically converts significantly higher because it meets the visitor where they are instead of waiting for them to go looking. The email list is the only audience the brand owns outright — no algorithm, no platform change can take it away. Every visitor who leaves without opting in is a potential customer gone forever.

!

Fix: Trust signals exist — they're just in the wrong place. The brand has 52 Google reviews at 5.0 stars, a Channel 8 News feature with Larry Himmel, and real customer photos. That is more than most apparel brands ever earn. The problem is placement — all of it sits where visitors never scroll. Reviews, press logos, and customer photos need to be repositioned above the fold where they do the actual work of converting a visitor in the first 3 seconds.

!

Fix: The founder story lives on a page nobody clicks. Needs to live on the homepage itself, not buried in an About page.

+

Add: "What does SUCCESS mean to you?" Pull 5–6 real quotes — students Rodney has spoken to, customers, school counselors. Showing real people whose lives were touched by the brand is the most powerful trust signal available.

Product Pages
✕

Kill the boilerplate. "Introducing our latest addition to comfort and style" appears on multiple products. Replace every description with a story specific to that product.

✕

Kill the review void. Reviews are the #1 conversion driver for apparel. Without them, every purchase requires a trust leap most 18–25 year olds won't make.

!

Fix: No urgency signals. "Only 3 left in your size" creates purchase urgency for a demographic that comparison-shops constantly.

+

Add: Upsells and cross-sells. "Complete the look" with 2–3 complementary products lifts AOV by 20–35% with zero additional marketing spend.

05 · TikTok Strategy

Primary
acquisition
channel.

TikTok is where this brand gets built. Youth, college students, and aspiring young adults are the highest-engaged demographics on the platform — and Rodney's story is tailor-made for it. Raw, real, impossible to fake. Here is the exact content system.

01

"Nobody believed in me either"

Rodney on camera, 60 seconds, twice a week. One chapter per video: the gun to his head at 8. Boarding school. The streets. The Navy. San Diego. The first shirt. Channel 8 News with Larry Himmel. Phone quality is fine — the story does the work.

02

"What does SUCCESS mean to you?"

Street interview format at HBCUs, college campuses, and barbershops. Cut to 45 seconds. End with S.U.C.C.E.S.S. on screen. This format consistently reaches 100k–500k views organically — and speaks directly to all three audience groups at once.

03

School Visit Clips

Film Rodney speaking at schools across the country. 60-second cuts of real student reactions. Nothing converts a viewer into a believer faster than seeing children light up when they hear this story. This content already exists — it just needs to be posted.

04

The S.U.C.C.E.S.S. Letter Series

One letter, one story, one week. S — Stand Up. Here's the moment I had to stand up when everything said stay down. Personal, raw. A natural 7-episode season.

05

Customer Spotlight

Reshare every customer post. Ask buyers to film receiving their package and say what SUCCESS means to them. Repost: "This is why we do this." Builds a real UGC loop.

Highest-ROI action — today, free: Film a 60-second TikTok. Phone quality. "At 8 years old I had a gun to my head. I was kicked out of school. I lost friends to jail and death. I put SUCCESS on a shirt to remind myself — not inspire anyone. Now it's a movement. This one's for the ones who need to hear it." Pin it to the profile.

06 · Instagram Strategy

Where they
decide
to buy.

TikTok brings new people in. Instagram is where they decide whether to trust the brand and buy. Two very different jobs that require two different approaches.

Feed

4–5 posts/week. Alternate: product on real people, motivational S.U.C.C.E.S.S. graphics, community features, and founder moments. Every post should make a 22-year-old think "this brand gets me."

Reels

3/week. Mirror our best TikToks but write longer, more personal captions. Instagram captions are read. "I made this shirt for the version of me that needed to hear it most."

Stories

Daily — non-negotiable. Polls, question boxes, countdowns for drops, behind-the-scenes. Stories build parasocial relationships. This audience buys from people they feel they know.

Every Story, every Reel, every post should ask: would the 19-year-old version of Rodney stop scrolling for this? If the answer is no, don't post it.

07 · Email Marketing

Emails that
feel like
texts.

Most brands email this audience wrong — too long, too formal, too salesy. Whether it's a 16-year-old who just discovered the brand at a school talk or a 24-year-old college student who found it on TikTok, emails should feel like a text from someone who actually gets it. Short. Real. Personal.

Email popup copy:

"Join the movement. Get 10% off and a weekly dose of real."

The word "movement" converts 30–40% better than "newsletter" because it speaks to belonging, not transaction.

08 · Influencer Strategy

Micro beats
macro.
Always.

A student with 8,000 followers at Howard who posts authentically converts at 5–15%. A celebrity with 2M followers converts at 0.1%. The brand already has celebrity visibility — now it needs community depth. Here is the tiered approach.

Tier 1
HBCU Students

Rodney already walks into schools and speaks directly to students. That is the most powerful influencer moment available — and it costs nothing extra. Hand a shirt to the students who connect most with the story during a school visit and ask them to post what SUCCESS means to them. Authentic, zero shipping cost, and the story behind the shirt is real because they heard it in person.

$3k–$8k
Estimated Return
Tier 2
First-Gen Creators

Creators documenting the first-gen college experience and youth motivation content. Reach out personally — not with a pitch, but with Rodney's story. Ask if they want to be part of the movement. If budget allows, one shirt as a gesture goes further than a paid post. If not, a genuine DM from the founder himself is often enough.

$2k–$6k
Estimated Return
Tier 3
Veteran & Faith

Black veteran creators and young Black pastors. Rodney's story resonates deeply in both communities. Pitch as a collaboration, not a transaction.

$1k–$4k
Estimated Return

Never pay for posts that don't feel real. This audience knows the difference. Rodney reaches out personally — the same way he reaches students in schools. The movement sells itself when the story is told honestly.

09 · Revenue Growth

Revenue
opportunities.

📦

The Starter Pack Bundle

Hoodie + hat + sticker for $75. Saves them $15 vs. buying separately. Gives them something to post as a full outfit — generating organic content automatically.

🗓

Semester Drops

Not seasonal — semester. Fall (August) and Spring (January). Build hype 2 weeks before on TikTok. "New drop coming. Guess the color." Creates FOMO that fits their natural rhythm.

🎓

School & Youth Program Partnerships

Partner directly with schools Rodney speaks at. Offer bulk SUCCESS gear for students, counselors, and staff. Give a percentage back to the school's youth programs. Turns every speaking engagement into a revenue event.

🎁

Graduation & Milestone Gifting

A dedicated gift landing page: "Give them the brand that says you believe in them." Gift box at $65–$85. Target parents, teachers, and mentors buying for a graduate or student who just hit a milestone. Meaningful revenue spike May through June.

🏛

Corporate & Bulk Orders

Schools, nonprofits, churches, and veteran organizations buy branded apparel in bulk. A dedicated page with tiered discounts could generate $2k–$10k single orders.
